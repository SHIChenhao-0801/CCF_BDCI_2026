\section{Introduction}
Research agents connect planning, experiment execution, analysis, and writing. Their apparent endpoint is often a manuscript, but a readable document can coexist with missing runtime artifacts, unresolved citations, or unusable accounting. An operational completion flag therefore answers a narrower question than whether an experiment is reproducible or its conclusion is correct. We address this gap with a small deterministic completion contract implemented in the JiuwenSwarm framework, rather than a second language model judging another model's prose.

The contract asks whether observable evidence is present: a nonempty ordinary PDF, a successful terminal Reporting report, safe existing declared paths, a parsable usage ledger, and resolvable compiled citation keys. It blocks publication of completion when one of these conditions fails. It deliberately does not certify that a scientific claim follows from an experiment or that a cited paper supports a sentence. These distinctions prevent a successful system test from being presented as a scientific result.

For a real execution case, we examine externally assisted reasoning. Chain-of-thought prompting motivates attention to inference-time context \citep{jason20224a94a8d1}, while iterative refinement raises a practical question: after an incorrect first answer, does a specific, automatically checkable error signal add value beyond simply asking the model to reconsider? Improvements over an initial answer alone confound information with an additional attempt. We therefore compare two selective retries that share the same initial answer and receive the same number of extra model calls. One adds a compact deterministic diagnostic; the other receives generic reconsideration text.

The resulting paired pilot is small and negative in its evidential conclusion: the diagnostic arm gains one item relative to the control, but a single discordant pair yields no significant paired evidence. The purpose is an inspectable measurement and system demonstration, not a claim of a new general reasoning algorithm. Our contributions are the framework completion gate, a traceable paired runner accepting legitimate alternative answers, and a bounded reporting integration that produces actual ICLR artifacts while retaining failure records. Topic, sources, protocol, data, and executable code were prepared with operator assistance; the system did not autonomously discover or generate them.
